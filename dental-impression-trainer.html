How to read one, step by step

  1. Remember it is a negative. Teeth stick out, so the impression has dents where they were. Cusp tips show up as low spots.
  2. Upper or lower? Use the clues in the table below.
  3. Find the handle and the midline. The handle sits at the front of the mouth, in line with the midline.
  4. Decide which side is the patient's right. Handle toward you means your left is the patient's right. Handle away from you means your left is the patient's left.
  5. Find the canine. It is the pointed, deep dent at the corner where the front turns into the side. It is your anchor.
  6. Count from the midline. Central, lateral, canine, two premolars, then up to three molars. Then use the Number finder tab to convert to the 1 to 32 number.

Quick check before it goes out

A front desk pass, not a clinical one. Follow your office and lab protocols, and the impression material's instructions.

Common problems

Void or bubble
A hole or bump in the material. It matters most on or near a tooth the lab needs.
Drag or tear
Smeared or ripped material, often from moving the tray while it set.
Tray show-through
The metal or plastic shows through the material on a tooth. It usually means not enough material there.
Pulled away from the tray
The material has separated from the tray, which can distort the impression.
Missing back areas
The last molars or the tuberosity or retromolar pad are not captured.
Wrong or missing label
No patient name, date, or arch. This is the easiest one to catch and fix.

If something looks off, show it to the dentist or assistant before sending. Do not decide on your own to retake.
